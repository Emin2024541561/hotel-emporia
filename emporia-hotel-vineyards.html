Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina

Essence over excess

Emporia sits above endless rows of vines outside Mostar, twelve rooms set into a piece of contemporary architecture that reads more like a private estate than a hotel. Below the guest floors, a working winery runs the length of the building — the reason the whole place exists.

Farm-to-table dining, a horizon pool that appears to spill into the valley, and a wine list written entirely by the house: Emporia is a slow argument for staying exactly where you are.

The Artisan winery

Nine wines, one archive tunnel, and a wine station whose history predates the country itself.

Imperial Vineyards

A wine school older than the border

The region's first wine station and wine school opened in 1866, under Emperor Franz Joseph I. The vineyards planted then are still known locally as the Imperial Vineyards, and Emporia's cellar carries their name forward.

The Concrete Eggs

Mineral wines, made without wood

Alongside oak barrique and clay amphora, part of the harvest ferments in concrete eggs — a shape that keeps the lees in constant, gentle motion and produces wines with a distinct mineral edge.

Deep Red & White Mist

Two cuvées, and a case for local grapes

Emporia's signature blends sit beside single-varietal bottlings of Žilavka and Blatina — the estate's argument that this corner of Herzegovina deserves its own place on a serious wine list.

Visit the Cellar

A tour, on foot, by quad, or by golf car

Guests can walk the vineyard rows, ride out by quad or golf cart, and finish underground in the archive tunnel where bottles age in amphora, concrete and toasted wood.

Helpful details for a smoother arrival.

01Are pets allowed?

Yes. Pets are welcome with a fee per stay. Only dogs and cats are allowed, and pets are not permitted in the pool or winery.

Emporia features 12 elegant rooms.

Guests can enjoy the horizon pool, restaurant, winery, wine tasting tunnel, event spaces, and a virtual tour.
